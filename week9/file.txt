1b. What prompt characteristics contribute to better energy efficiency?
  -Conciseness and Precision: 
    Shorter prompts that are still highly specific require fewer compute cycles and less GPU power, directly reducing energy use.
  -Zero-Shot or Few-Shot Techniques: 
    Utilizing zero-shot (no examples) or simple one-shot approaches is generally more energy-efficient than complex few-shot prompts.
  -Use of Explicit Constraints: 
    Clearly defining expectations in the prompt reduces the need for the model to generate extra tokens and decreases 
    the necessity for subsequent, energy-heavy revisions.
  -Structured Formatting/Tags: 
    Using structured formatting or custom tags, such as providing context within defined sections, 
    can guide the model to more direct answers, lowering energy consumption.
  -Iterative Refinement: 
    Rather than blindly prompting multiple times to get a good result, designing a robust, thoughtful, and reusable prompt structure 
    saves total energy. 

1c. Do the energy-optimal prompt characteristics vary across genAI instances (products)?
  Yes, energy-optimal prompt characteristics vary significantly across different generative AI instances (products), as energy consumption 
  is heavily dependent on the model's architecture, size, and the specific hardware on which it runs. 
  While certain practices, like minimizing output length, are generally beneficial, the specific phrasing, keyword choices, and prompting 
  techniques that minimize energy consumption must be tailored to the particular model being used.

2. Three pieces of of information you should not type into prompts in order to preserve privacy, both your own and that of others
  -Personally Identifiable Information (PII): 
    This includes full names, home addresses, Social Security numbers, passport numbers, email addresses, 
    phone numbers, or any, or any other data that can be used to identify you or another person.
  -Financial and Sensitive Information: 
    Avoid inputting credit card numbers, bank account details, investment portfolios, tax identification numbers, 
    or confidential medical records/diagnostic information.
  -Confidential Workplace or Proprietary Data: 
    Never paste company confidential information, source code, non-public legal documents, or trade secrets into a prompt. 
    This can violate employer policies or legal agreements.
